Small-business loan default: a scorecard tested the way a lender would use it

Predicting early charge-off of SBA 7(a) loans approved after the model was built, benchmarked against the lender's own price for risk, with the checks a model-risk team runs.

Andres PerezPython · DuckDB · scikit-learn · R ShinyCode: github.com/Andresperez397/sba-loan-default-risk

Data

514,984 disbursed SBA 7(a) loans approved FY2010–FY2020 (SBA loan-level FOIA data, public domain). The outcome is a charge-off within five years of approval. Test cohorts FY2015–FY2020 (304,289 loans) are each scored by a model trained only on loans five or more years older, matching what a lender could have known.

Approach

  • Audit, then a frozen plan; borrower names and addresses never read.
  • Leak caught: SBA overwrites the term of loans that went bad (AUC 0.86 on its own), so term was removed.
  • Models: weight-of-evidence scorecard (points, reason codes) against gradient boosting and an interest-rate baseline.
  • Policy and model risk: approval trade-offs in dollars, stability (PSI), calibration and segments.

Findings

  • The scorecard beats boosting out of time (AUC 0.684 against 0.677), even though boosting won in-time cross-validation.
  • The edge over pricing is modest: +0.052 AUC against the absolute rate, but most of that was the prime rate rising. Against the rate's spread over prime, +0.010 (exploratory).
  • Policy: the scorecard cuts the count of early charge-offs at 70–75% approval, but in lender dollars declining by rate is as good or slightly better, because the scorecard declines many small loans.
  • Escalations: rate, guarantee and program drift; FY2020 over-predicted (COVID relief).

Limitations

The outcome counts early charge-offs only (52–60% of charge-offs to date). There is no real-estate flag once term is removed, and no lender identity. Public data has no credit-bureau or financial-statement fields, so these results describe what origination data alone can do. The spread check came after seeing results and is labelled exploratory.

Data: U.S. Small Business Administration 7(a) FOIA data, snapshot as of June 30, 2026 (U.S. Government Works); prime rate from FRED (DPRIME). Not affiliated with or endorsed by the SBA.
